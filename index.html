THE A7i STANDARD

Trust is built by what we refuse to oversimplify.

The public site should not show our internal commercial flywheel. It should show the rules A7i follows when translating supplement science for people.

01Evidence over hype.

We separate established roles, promising research and unsupported marketing.

02Context before claims.

Form, dose, population and outcome stay attached to the evidence.

03Food first where possible.

We show what food can realistically provide before reaching for a bottle.

04A clinician when it matters.

Diagnosis, medicines, pregnancy and individual risk require professional advice.

WHEN TO TAKE IT

Timing, without the rituals.

Every A7i ingredient page will answer four separate questions: Does time of day matter? Does food matter? Does timing around exercise matter? and what does the research actually support?

If research does not prove a “best time”, A7i will say exactly that.
Mineral

Magnesium

Best-supported timingNo clearly proven best time of day.

Bedtime is popular, especially in sleep products, but current human evidence does not establish bedtime dosing as superior. If magnesium is being used in relation to sleep, A7i should distinguish the sleep evidence from the timing claim itself.

Performance · Cellular energy

Creatine

Best-supported timingTake it consistently; pre vs post-workout is not settled.

Some small studies have suggested a post-workout advantage, but reviews conclude the evidence is too limited to make a firm timing rule. For most users, consistently reaching the intended daily intake matters more than chasing a narrow workout window.

Time of dayFlexible
Around exerciseInconclusive
What matters moreConsistency

Vitamin · Fat soluble

Vitamin D

Best-supported timingWith a meal containing some fat.

Human absorption studies support taking vitamin D with food, and dietary fat can increase vitamin D3 absorption. Evidence is much stronger for the meal context than for a specific morning-versus-evening rule.

Time of dayNo proven winner
With food?Yes, supported
Timing confidenceModerate
Show me the timing evidence Randomised study: dietary fat and vitamin D3 absorption ↗ Study: vitamin D with the largest meal ↗
Fatty acid / lipid

Omega-3

Best-supported timingTake with a meal; formulation changes how much food matters.

EPA/DHA bioavailability is influenced by chemical form and delivery system. Food can materially improve absorption for some formulations, particularly conventional ethyl-ester products. There is no universal evidence-based “morning” or “night” rule.

Time of dayFlexible
With food?Usually sensible
What matters moreFormulation + meal
Show me the timing evidence Review: improving omega-3 bioavailability ↗ Human study: food effects on EPA/DHA formulation ↗
Mineral · Clinical context

Iron

Absorption-focused timingMorning, away from meals and coffee, can improve absorption.

In iron-deficient women, absorption was higher in the morning than afternoon and markedly reduced by coffee and breakfast. Alternate-day dosing can also improve fractional absorption in some settings. Iron is different from casual wellness supplements: the need, dose and regimen should be clinically guided.

Time of dayMorning favoured
Food/coffeeCan reduce absorption
Clinical oversightImportant
Show me the timing evidence Human isotope study: dietary factors and time of day ↗ Iron absorption: consecutive vs alternate-day dosing ↗
Do not use this card to self-diagnose iron deficiency. Ask a qualified healthcare professional whether supplementation and testing are appropriate.
Microbiome · Strain specific

Probiotics

Best-supported answerThere is no universal timing rule for “probiotics”.

Timing depends on organism, strain, formulation and coating. An in-vitro gastrointestinal model of one multi-strain product found better bacterial survival when taken with or about 30 minutes before a meal than 30 minutes after. That is useful formulation evidence, not a universal clinical rule for every probiotic.

Time of dayProduct specific
Meal timingMay matter
Timing confidenceStrain/form dependent
Show me the timing evidence Study: impact of meals on probiotic survival ↗
A7i timing rule: “When to take it” will never be copied from generic supplement folklore. Each ingredient will be classified as timing matters, meal matters, consistency matters more, or evidence insufficient, with the study window directly underneath.

A7i Supplements provides general educational information only. It is not medical advice and is not a substitute for diagnosis, treatment or advice from a qualified healthcare professional. HEALTH FIRST

Use A7i to ask better questions — not to replace your clinician.

A7i cannot know your complete medical history, diagnoses, medicines, allergies, pregnancy status, test results or individual nutritional needs.

  • Always ask your doctor, pharmacist or qualified healthcare professional before starting or changing a supplement.
  • Do not stop prescribed medicines because of information on this site.
  • Seek individual advice for pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, surgery, chronic disease or medication interactions.
  • Evidence summaries describe research; they are not guarantees that a product will work for you.
